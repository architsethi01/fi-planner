How the numbers are produced

Tax engines use published 2026 parameters. United States: federal married-filing-jointly brackets, $32,200 standard deduction, Social Security wage base and Medicare surtax; state and local income tax are not modelled. India: new-regime slabs for FY 2026-27, ₹75,000 standard deduction, Section 87A rebate, surcharge tiers and 4% health and education cess; the old regime is not modelled. United Kingdom: England, Wales and Northern Ireland bands, £12,570 personal allowance with taper above £100,000, and employee National Insurance; Scottish rates differ. Payroll and social-security treatment is approximated, and no jurisdiction's rules are modelled completely.

Historical returns are drawn from published index data and long-run studies; they are past results, not forecasts. A twenty-year average conceals very wide year-to-year variation, and over horizons shorter than roughly fifteen years the order in which good and bad years arrive affects outcomes as much as the average does. Cost-of-living multipliers are approximate planning indices, not precise measurements. Projections assume constant real income, constant real returns and no market crashes, none of which happen in reality.
